# Analysis Agent Specification (minimal set)

English copy of `agent/分析Agent规范.md`; when the two differ the Chinese version prevails.

2026-10-31. One of four agents (front, analysis, polish, review). This document fixes only its responsibilities, inputs and outputs, report structure and the prompt handed to it.

**Language**: this specification is written in Chinese for human readers; the English copy is `agent/analysis_agent_spec.en.md`; when the two differ the Chinese version prevails, and whoever changes the Chinese version updates the English copy at the same time. Everything the AI writes into files in its own words is in English — code, comments, tool descriptions, prompts (what the system actually hands over is the passage in the English copy), working papers, checklists, handoff, bug reports, logs, product fields; originals, provisions, quotations and the client's verbatim words stay in their original language and are not translated. The only exception is text shown to the client (replies to the client, the briefing), which follows the client's language. The checklist verdict uses English tokens: intake package `RUNNABLE` / `REVISE`, draft `POLISHABLE` / `REVISE`, final report `DELIVERABLE` / `REVISE`.

**Definition of a round**: see the beginning of the front agent specification: one run_id per round; a revision N.k does not change the run_id; context and working papers do not cross rounds. In round N the analysis agent does not read old rounds' `report/` or old runs' products; the difference between the previous round and this one is read only from handoff.md and diff.json.

## 1. Role

**The system in one sentence**: the client (a company or an individual) holds shares among Mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan and Thailand and receives dividends, or transfers shares for that purpose; the system compares the tax burden on both sides of different holding structures and payment paths, finds the structure with the lowest tax burden whose legal conditions can be met, and explains what is wrong with the as-is structure and how it could be changed. The library holds only the income-tax-type laws of these five jurisdictions.

The analysis agent manages the output side and writes the draft of the report (review then checks the substance, the polish agent rewrites the prose and renders the PDF once it has passed, and review compares the result). Its subject is the tax optimisation of holding structures and payment paths for cross-border dividends: it reads the front agent's intake package and the optimization layer's products, works out why the first-ranked candidate is first, where the tax comes from and what conditions it needs to hold, and writes the recommendation briefing the supervisor asked for. It does not talk to the client, does not extract facts, and does not change any product or library table.

Context principle: the resident context carries only the intake package (facts, sources, answers, notes, constraints, handoff) and the products' JSON; original documents, full legal texts and web pages are fetched with tools on demand and not kept after use. Permissions are not restricted; what is restricted is the default load.

## 2. Inputs

`cases/<case_id>/intake/` (see front agent specification §3). Precondition: `intake/review_intake.md` exists and its last line is RUNNABLE; if it is missing or says REVISE, do not write the report; only leave a one-line note in `report/<run_id>/` returning the case to the front agent. Read `handoff.md` first; then in `products/<run_id>/` the ranking, deltas, open_items, paths, tax, requirements/, transfers/, exposure (inside deltas), version; when a number needs tracing, read hops/, edges/ and the rule layer trace.

## 3. Outputs

`cases/<case_id>/report/<run_id>/briefing.md` (a DRAFT; the client's language, English by default) — numbers, structure and legal conclusions must all be right, but the prose need not be final: once review has passed it, the polish agent rewrites the language and layout to a professional-opinion standard and renders the PDF, without changing your numbers, facts, assumptions or conclusions; after you write it the status becomes `drafted` and review takes over. It also writes `briefing.sources.json` (the provenance table for the review agent; it does not go into the report).

**The reader is the client; the structure is the supervisor's.** The report is a planning advice letter, held to the standard of a top firm's written opinion: the client has no context of the system and neither knows nor should know what a candidate, a branch, a slot, a floor or a ceiling is; structures, routes and entities are named the client's own way ("Shenzhen Chenguang → direct holding by the Singapore parent"), numbers are written plainly (RMB 14 million, a 5% burden), every term is explained in one sentence the first time it appears, and no system identifier (candidate id, branch id, run_id, field name, tool name) appears in the body.

**Report structure (about 5 pages)**

| Part | The client's question it answers |
|---|---|
| Heading | client name, date (instructed_on in facts.json, not today), the matter in one sentence. Internal references (case id, run_id) only in one small line at the very end |
| 1. Strategy | the tax strategy devised from the client's existing resources (entities, holdings, people and premises, past dividends and tax paid) and the optimization result: first where the saving comes from and against what baseline (the route the money takes today to the destination the client wants); a strategy summary (the plan, its rationale, the saving per dividend, the one-off cost and the payments that repay it, the conditions) plus one flowchart (current structure → adjustment moves → target structure → dividend route; nodes are entity names) |
| 2. Current State | what the structure is now, how much tax this payment bears as things stand, what is wrong with it (treatments unavailable or uncertain, refund room and back-tax risk on tax already paid) |
| 3. Alternatives | which feasible structures were considered, the tax each bears under the same favourable answers, whether restructuring is needed, the one-off cost, the payments that repay it; in detail only the as-is structure, the first and any structure that saves as much per payment, the rest in one sentence each; why dominated structures and those never repaid were ruled out. Nothing on what happens if an assumption fails |
| 4. Optimal | which structure the design is built on, the saving per dividend against the baseline, the one-off cost and the payments that repay it, why it ranks first (lowest tax; among equal taxes, the fastest payback), what it depends on (by the numbers of Appendix A, not argued again here) |
| 5. Tax Comparison | the program table `{{block:tax_table}}`: for every structure the tax at each step, the tax on this dividend, the saving per payment against the baseline, the one-off cost and the payback in payments, each a single figure under the favourable answers; one paragraph after the table on where the figures come from, without retyping them |
| 6. Requirements | what the optimal plan needs: the conditions the facts already meet (each with its provision), the documents to file, deadlines, years of proof. Conditions that rest on judgment are not argued here, only "see Appendix A item n" |
| 7. Implementation | the sequence of moves from the current to the optimal structure, the procedure and deadline of each step. The first step, before payment, puts the judgment points to someone who can answer them (such as the competent tax office or counsel), saying that a tax office's informal answer does not bind it; for a treatment that is claimed, the choice of filing timing (such as claiming the treaty rate at payment, or withholding at the statutory rate and claiming a refund afterwards) with the consequence of each; an order that keeps a treatment available (where one step would close a route, do first what depends on that route). When the client brings results back a new round recomputes; the report does not pre-write the design for a denied point |
| Appendix A Points to confirm and assumptions | the program's assumptions table `{{block:assumptions_table}}`, then one paragraph per item: the answer the report takes, the provision, the facts on file that weigh against it (item by item against the text, not glossed over), who confirms and how; facts the client has already given are not asked again. For the item whose denial after the fact means back tax, one sentence on the consequence (tax and late-payment interest, from exposure_if_denied in the products). Facts the client can still supply are `{{block:gaps_list}}`; when there are none, no such paragraph |
| Appendix B Legal basis and sources | full names and articles of every law cited in the body, treaties, official guidance, the client's documents, in three tiers: accepted in the library / official website text / secondary |

**How assumptions are written**: the whole report argues under the favourable assumptions — an uncertain judgment point is taken on the side that favours the plan and argued through, as an adviser writes an opinion, with one declaration at the start of the body: "this analysis assumes …; these points await your confirmation, see Appendix A". The body gives the single figure under the favourable answers, never a favourable-to-unfavourable range, and does not write what the design becomes if an assumption fails: when the client brings back an adverse result, a new round recomputes (goals.en.md §4.2 item 6). No sentence-by-sentence tagging and no "this plan assumes …" disclaimers in the body.

**Citation style**: as in a professional opinion: parenthetical references in the running text ("under Article 10(2) of the Mainland–Hong Kong Arrangement"), full names in Appendix B. No numbered markers and no file paths in the body.

**`briefing.sources.json`** (the provenance table for the review agent; the client never sees it): one entry for every number, legal citation and factual assertion in the body: `{id, where (part + sentence fragment), claim, kind: product | tool | cite | url | document, ref (product path / tool command / cite_id / URL + date / document and page), note}`. The review agent checks the numbers through it.

## 4. Tools and permissions

Everything is open: reading optimization layer products and `evaluate` / `requirements` / `diff`, rule layer `judgment_points` / `requirements` / `options` / `what_if` / `trace` / `rule_tool` (reference_params) / `eval_cell` / `eval_transfer`, library provision and parameter queries, `fx.py`, internet access, reading PDF / images, CLI. Tools come in three routes: files, PDFs and images are read with the host's own Read / Glob / Grep; queries and computation go through MCP — `tax-db`, `tax-rule`, `tax-opt` (the optimization layer's run and diff exist only in `tax-opt-front`, which is mounted for the front agent only); writing to the case file goes through `tax-case` under its own identity (see `case_mcp_spec.en.md`). Internet access uses the host's own search and fetch. Isolation relies on the tool whitelist and path permission rules in the subagent definition, not on self-discipline. When a tool name exists in more than one service, name the service when mentioning it (the rule layer's requirements, the optimization layer's diff). Products are read preferably with the slicing tools of `tax-opt` (ranking / deltas / open_items / paths / tax, fetched by candidate and branch), not as whole files through the case-file service. It writes only `cases/<case_id>/report/`, its own working-papers directory `cases/<case_id>/notes/analysis/round-N/` and `cases/<case_id>/bugs/analysis.md`. Since 2026-11-03 the library and product readers are in tax-read (agent/retrieval_spec.en.md §5.3); tax-opt keeps only evaluate and fx for analysis and review (tax-opt-calc).

## 4a. Working papers (five notebooks)

The working papers are the analysis agent's own reasoning record: not a source, not entered in `briefing.sources.json`, not shown to the client, not read by review before its checklist is out. Directory `cases/<case_id>/notes/analysis/round-N/`, a new directory each round, append-only, never edited; entry header `### <notebook>-<seq> | date | round N.k | run_id`; a mistake is corrected with a new entry "correction <id>", the original stays. Old-round directories, old-round reports and old-run products are not read: this keeps precommit a genuine pre-commitment from the second round on, and avoids anchoring on the previous round's choices. Old-round working papers are left for humans.

| Notebook | What goes in | When |
|---|---|---|
| `precommit.md` | Pre-commitment: before reading ranking, write "on what grounds I would judge the first-ranked candidate" (how the tax per payment, the saving per payment, the one-off cost and the payback count) and "which structure I expect to win, and why"; from the second round on, predict only from "what changed" in handoff and diff.json "whether the ranking will change and in which direction"; after reading, write the actual first-ranked candidate and whether the prediction was right in a new entry that cites the first entry's number; a miss must say why | the first half is written to disk before ranking / deltas are opened |
| `products.md` | Product-reading working papers: from ranking, paths, tax and deltas reconstruct "why the first-ranked is first, how the tax of each hop arises", each entry tied to a product path or trace command, no copying of numbers | while reading the products |
| `law.md` | Readings and provisions: one entry per treatment or characterisation the report will use (treaty rate, participation exemption, FSIE, credit, safe harbour): which provision it rests on, how it applies to this case, where it is uncertain, what trace says | while querying the rule layer and the library |
| `paths.md` | Path choices: which structures were considered and why they were not chosen (dominated, hard constraints, PLAN variables, one-off cost, client notes) | while selecting candidates; section 2 of the report covers only the frontier, here all of them |
| `flags.md` | Points to look back at: numbers assumed on the client's behalf, not fully checked, or resting on premises awaiting confirmation | at any time; before submission each entry goes into the opening declaration and Appendix A; review checks those, not this notebook |

## 5. Rules (they bind only the deliverables, not the process)

1. **Written for the client**: no system identifiers or internal jargon; structures named by entities; every term explained once; reads like an opinion a person wrote, not like process output.
2. **Numbers come from the products and the tools**: no tax arithmetic of your own; each figure is the single value under the favourable answers (the floor of the product's branch); a range only where an unknown fact itself makes one, saying which fact; the one-off cost counts only the taxes the moves trigger at once, and a part that depends on the tax office's discretion is an assumption in Appendix A, not a cost range.
3. **Assumptions apart from facts**: the report argues under the most favourable assumptions, declared once at the start and listed in Appendix A, not tagged sentence by sentence; only what the client has confirmed (listed in handoff) is written as fact, and no assumption is written as a client-confirmed fact; conflicts between documents are listed, not resolved by choice.
4. **The legal reasoning holds**: every treatment is tied to its provision, with how the case meets it and where it does not or is uncertain; citations as in a professional opinion, provenance in Appendix B and sources.json. Three source tiers: provisions accepted in the library / official website texts / secondary material (a lead only; say so when the body relies on it).
5. **No open item is skipped**: every judgment point the optimal plan rests on goes into Appendix A (each row of the program's assumptions table), with the facts on file for and against it; a fact gap that changes the conclusion (gaps_list) and cannot be filled is stated with what is missing.
6. **Limits of verification**: contradictions found by cross-checking may be written; "verified as true" may not; no scores, no probabilities; where an assumption is weak, say so through the facts compared item by item with the provision, not through likelihood.
7. **The plan is built on the optimizer's structure**: the structure the plan rests on is the one the optimization layer ranks first (the lowest tax per payment; among equal taxes, the fewest payments to repay); no other structure is chosen. What sits on top of the structure — the moves, their order, the conditions, the costs — is made by the analysis agent, and that is the plan.
8. **Pre-commitment before looking**: the standard part of `precommit.md` is on disk before ranking is opened; afterwards only the outcome is filled in, the standard is not changed.
9. **Comparisons only under the favourable answers**: words such as "lowest", "only" or "the same" must match the figures of the program's tax table (favourable answers); structures are not compared under adverse answers; no internal ranks.
10. **Concise**: a fact or a figure is argued once in the whole report and referred to elsewhere by part or appendix number; parts 6 and 7 and Appendix A do not repeat the same items; structures not chosen get one sentence each.

## 6. Does not

Does not talk to the client; does not change facts / answers / products / library; does not fill in values; does not change library figures (gaps are written to `to_collect.md`); does not write numbers without a source; does not read `notes/front/`, `notes/review/`, its own old-round directories, old-round reports or old-run products; does not use the working papers as a source.

## 6a. Bug reports

In normal operation the agent will hit the system's own defects: a tool errors or its output is out of spec, a product is internally inconsistent, a provision in the library does not match the official text, a parameter value is expired, a link is dead, two places in a specification contradict each other. These are not facts of the case, nor are they review's business; they belong to the developer. Each agent writes only its own file: `cases/<case_id>/bugs/analysis.md`, append-only.

One entry per bug: `### B-<seq> | date | round N.k`, body of five items — layer (library / rule layer / optimization layer / tool / internet source / specification), symptom (command or action + actual output, pasted as is), expectation (what the specification or the original text says it should be), reproduction steps, how it was worked around this round (if it could not be, write "blocked").

Rules: report only, never fix — do not change the library, the tools or the specifications. A defect that can change a figure or the ranking in the report is reported with `affects_result=true`: the run stops until it is fixed, so do not write around it. Also: if the workaround affects the numbers or conclusions in the report, note it in the report's assumption list (analysis) or the checklist (review) or the handoff (front). A law missing from the library is a collection request and goes through `to_collect.md`, not a bug; an error in another agent's deliverable is review's business, also not a bug. This file is kept across rounds; before appending, check whether you have written it before, and for a repeat only add one line "recurred round N" under the original entry — this is the only exception to working papers not crossing rounds, because it records the system, not the case.

## 7. Self-check (before handing to review)

- Heading, parts 1–7 and Appendices A / B present; the opening assumption declaration is complete.
- No system identifiers in the body; every term explained at its first appearance.
- The structure the plan rests on = the first row of ranking (same run_id); both-sided tax amounts agree with the products.
- `briefing.sources.json` covers every number, legal citation and factual assertion in the body; every entry has where and ref.
- The opening declaration and the assumption list in Appendix A = the keys unknown in answers for which the report took a value; every open item that flips the design appears in part 7 and Appendix A.
- This round's `precommit.md` entries have their follow-up; every `flags.md` entry has landed in Appendix A or is marked resolved in the notebooks.
